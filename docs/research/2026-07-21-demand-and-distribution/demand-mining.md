## Decision rule

Public data can establish **repeated, costly problem exposure**. It cannot establish willingness to buy your product. Treat a pattern as a candidate only when it clears all three gates:

1. Repetition: independently expressed by **≥10 people or organizations** in a defined ICP over 90 days.
2. Cost: **≥3 describe a concrete workaround**—manual spreadsheet, custom script, contractor, headcount, paid tool stack, or delayed deployment.
3. Cross-source corroboration: it appears in **≥2 source types** (for example GitHub issues + jobs; Reddit + review complaints).

Those are practical anti-self-deception thresholds, not scientifically validated universal cutoffs. The published literature does not provide a proven “N mentions equals viable startup” threshold.

## What to automate first

Build a **candidate ledger**, not an “idea score”:

- One row per primary source item: URL, date, person/org, ICP, problem verbatim, workflow, workaround, consequence, incumbent, buyer evidence, and source type.
- Deduplicate at the person/org level before counting.
- Cluster only after manual labeling 30–50 items; LLM clustering is useful for recall but should not determine the cluster.
- Score candidates only on: distinct organizations, workaround count, cross-source count, recency, and evidence of an economic buyer.
- Fail candidates quickly: if a cluster cannot clear repetition + cost + two sources in a week of public research, do not promote it to build work.

A useful hard distinction: “people complain about product X” is competitive intelligence; “teams repeatedly perform job Y with a script/spreadsheet because X and Z fail” is a potential product thesis.

## Existing pain-mining products: what they are actually good for

- [Common Room](https://www.commonroom.io/docs/signals/) is the most credible broad commercial category: it connects owned/community signals including Discord, GitHub, Reddit, Slack, Discourse, and G2, then routes/scoring them for GTM. It is good at **monitoring known communities and accounts**; it is not an independent validator of an unknown market, and its useful data is mostly data you already have permission to connect.
- [Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/about?lang=en) is useful for trend discovery/search-adjacent hypothesis generation. Its API advertises **60 requests/minute** ([API docs](https://api.explodingtopics.com/)). Its “millions of data points” and “12+ months early” positioning are vendor claims; I found no independent benchmark showing that its surfaced topics predict B2B willingness to pay.
- GummySearch was a focused Reddit-research product, but it [closed on 30 November 2025](https://gummysearch.com/pricing/). Its last published plans were $0 / $29 / $59 / $199 per month, and its release notes said it depended on Pushshift for comment history. That closure is a concrete warning about data-access dependence, not evidence that demand research is unprofitable.
- [PainPointer](https://www.painpointer.com/) and similar “AI pain point” tools can accelerate query expansion and triage. They are discovery interfaces, not validation engines. I found no audited accuracy, precision/recall, conversion-lift, or downstream-revenue benchmark for them.
- Review-analytics vendors can cluster feedback well **when you own or license the corpus**. They do not solve the central public-data problem: the major B2B review sites gate APIs or prohibit automated extraction. Any vendor claiming broad automated G2/Capterra/TrustRadius mining should be asked for its licensing path before you rely on it.

## What the evidence does and does not support

There is real evidence that large technical conversation corpora contain useful behavioral signal: one Stack Overflow dataset study analyzed **3.5 million questions, 6.9 million answers, and 1.3 million users** ([study](https://research.google/pubs/analysis-of-the-reputation-system-and-user-contributions-on-a-question-answering-website-stackoverflow/)); GitHub issue research regularly analyzes thousands of projects. That supports automated *discovery and triage*.

It does **not** support the common Lean Startup claim that public complaints are sufficient validation. I found no credible controlled study that compares “pain-mined ideas” with a matched non-mined set and reports a reliable lift in paid conversion, revenue, retention, or survival. The correct output of mining is a ranked list of testable hypotheses and named prospects—not a green light to build.

## WHAT I COULD NOT FIND

- A reliable published threshold such as “10 Reddit complaints,” “500 monthly searches,” or “30% unanswered Stack Overflow questions” that predicts paid B2B demand. Numbers used above are operating gates to reduce false positives, not established laws.
- A controlled, replicated study showing that pain-mining tools or LLM complaint clustering improve startup conversion, revenue, retention, or survival versus careful manual research.
- Credible, independently audited accuracy/precision/recall benchmarks for GummySearch alternatives, PainPointer-type tools, or “AI product idea” tools.
- A public, stable price sheet or open-research API for competitor review text across G2, Capterra, and TrustRadius. G2/TrustRadius offer gated customer/API routes; Capterra’s terms expressly forbid the proposed automated mining without written consent.
- A legal, general-purpose public API for LinkedIn job-search corpus mining. Its official job API is vetted and designed for postings, while its terms prohibit scraping.
- Generalizable conversion, response-rate, or timeline benchmarks from public signal → interview → paid pilot for zero-audience technical founders. Published numbers are mostly vendor marketing, small self-reported founder anecdotes, or incomparable outbound datasets.
- Evidence that high search volume means a startup opportunity. It can represent information seeking, free-solution hunting, education, job hunting, or incumbent-brand traffic; sources consistently treat it as advertiser planning data, not purchase intent.
