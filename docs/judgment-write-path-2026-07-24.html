The judgment write path

Every write to judgment canon runs the same gauntlet. Only intent-backed writes cross a publication boundary — and that boundary is what decides whether a failure means nothing happened or it happened, the surface is just behind.

lib/judgment-writer.js — runOp · publishIntentWith · commitWithProjections

Why the clear comes before the regenerate

if reversedRegenerate first, then clear, and a crash between them leaves a published-looking set of surfaces sitting on top of a live intent. The next replay re-applies a write the reader has already seen.
if collapsedTreat a projection failure after the clear as an ordinary failure, and the compensating rollback reverts committed canon — a durable, attested write undone because a Markdown file could not be opened.
soThe clear is the one durable boundary. Everything before it rolls back and keeps the intent; everything after it repairs forward and never rolls back.

The same asymmetry is why replay sits at the head of reads as well as writes. A surface that is behind has no separate repair path — the next person to look at it fixes it by looking.

JUDGMENT_PROJECTION_STALE means behind, not failed — collapsing it into an ordinary failure is how committed canon gets rolled back over a file nobody could write.

published — canon written, surfaces current refused — nothing published, intent retained, safe to retry stale — published, surfaces behind, repairs on next read

Ordinary writes take a shorter road: mutate the records, regenerate, and on any failure restore and re-regenerate under JUDGMENT_PARTIAL_WRITE. They never cross a publication boundary because they have no intent to clear — which is exactly why transitions and the goal migration are intent-backed instead.
