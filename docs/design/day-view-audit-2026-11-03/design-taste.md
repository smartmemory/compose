# design-taste audit of the Day view (2026-11-03)

Method: skill `design-taste-frontend` (tasteskill v2) loaded first. The Playwright MCP browser was locked by a parallel auditor ("Browser is already in use"), so the screens were taken with the skill's own Chrome driver (`scripts/lib/cdp.mjs`) at exact viewports, measured `innerWidth` 1440x900 and 400x800, after a 15 s settle: `design-taste-1440.png`, `design-taste-400.png`. The skill's inspector (`inspect.mjs --widths 1440,400`) was also run against the live page: 9 FAIL, 6 WARN (the ones that apply are folded into the findings below, the ones that do not are listed at the end). The `/api/ribbon` feed was read once to check one mark. No source edits, no server touched.

## What the screen is for (2 lines, your reading)
The owner opens Compose and must know in five seconds what moved, what is stuck, and what is waiting on them, then where the day goes next.
The ribbon is the answer laid out in time: one row per lane, the past on the left of the now line, the current unit and its ETA on the right.

## Three things it does well
- The idea comes from the subject, not the category. The now line is the hinge of the page: the past drawn as spans, the present and next unit written in words to its right, and AHEAD holding resets in the future. It passes the skill's competitor test, a generic ops dashboard would not have it.
- The phone is its own composition, not a squeezed ribbon (`DayPhone.jsx`). It drops the timeline and gives a readable list of lanes in full sentences, with no sideways scroll (inspector: 0 overflow at 400px).
- The words are plain and owner-shaped: "waiting on review 3d 18h", "Is the process improving?", "How far is 2.0 from flipping?". The legend and the bars share one pattern source (`Patterns`), so they can never drift, and both themes are defined in one token block.

## One-paragraph direction
On this page, the owner's day becomes a timetable whose now line is the hinge, and everything should serve that one reading. Make the WHAT line the single large thing on the screen (figures at 28 to 32px, the words beside them at body size), give "on you" the one accent colour and nothing else, and let every other state step down to flat tones of the ground so the ribbon reads as quiet grey with a few bright marks that mean "act". Remove what decorates rather than informs: the boxes and borders around the panels, the hatching on the least actionable state, the repeated project chips, the tracked uppercase labels and the mono figures. Then fix the three places where the drawing tells a wrong story (the whole-field "not recorded" box, the ETA pinned to the edge, and question marks in the headline). The result should look less like a monitoring console and more like a printed railway timetable for one person's day: one face, two sizes, one accent, and nothing on it that does not answer "what is waiting on me, and when does it move".

## Where the skill does not fit
- The skill says of itself "Not for dashboards or app screens". Its draw step (directions, sectors, section blueprints, a copy deck) is built for a marketing page and was not run: there is no brand to choose, no scroll story, no sections. Only the house rules, the laws of type, colour and space, and the look loop were applied.
- Inspector rules that do not apply here and were dropped: `few-pictures`, `no-real-pictures`, `no-footage` (an operations cockpit should carry no photographs or video), `still-page` and the motion layer (entrance animation on a screen opened many times a day is a cost, not a gain), `no-hero` and `first-action` (the WHAT line is the hero, there is no single call to action), and the 16 to 20px body size (dense app rows at 13px are normal, 13px stays the floor).
- Rules applied with an exemption: lines on real form fields (the two date inputs) and on the timeline axis and gridlines, which are data, not ornament. The house ban on monospace and tracked caps is the owner's taste for websites. On this screen it is applied as a recommendation (findings 10 and 14, MED and LOW), not as a hard fail.
