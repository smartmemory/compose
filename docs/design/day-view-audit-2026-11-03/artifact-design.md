# artifact-design audit of the Day view (2026-11-03)

## What the screen is for (2 lines, your reading)
One glance at the start of a session should tell the owner which lanes are moving, which are stuck, and which are stuck on him, plus how much seat and dollar budget is left.
Everything else on the screen (range controls, legend, links down) is there to support that one read.

## Three things it does well
- The ribbon's state colors are real tokens with a matching `.dark .day-ribbon` override (`DayRibbon.jsx:21-25`), and the legend swatches reuse the bar patterns, so the legend cannot drift from the marks.
- Below 768px it drops the chart for a plain "Now" list and a meter instead of shrinking the ribbon, and the 400px shot has no sideways scroll (document width measured at 400).
- Motion is restrained and respects `prefers-reduced-motion` (the lane flash falls back to an outline, `DayRibbon.jsx:79`), and the WHAT counts use `tabular-nums`.

## One-paragraph direction
Treat the ribbon as a pixel-true instrument, not a scaled picture: one SVG unit per CSS pixel, a fixed label column and ETA gutter, text measured rather than counted, and lanes that scroll rather than shrink. Then make the palette say one thing at a glance. Neutrals and accent come from one token set shared by app and ribbon in both themes, tan means "waiting on review", and a single distinct hue and form means "waiting on you", the one state the owner must act on. Cut the type to three sizes, keep mono for identifiers only, and remove repeated window labels so the summary line, the chart and the budget meter are the only three things competing for the eye. On phone, keep the list approach and fix the gutter and the clipped meter.
